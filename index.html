About Me

Hobbyist Independent Developer

It started with toys like Lego. My castles were always small and lacklustre — not because I was short on ideas, but because I was short on bricks, and I couldn't exactly ask my parents for more. The limit was never my imagination; it was the pile of bricks I had.

Computers changed that. In a sandbox game, I could build a castle as big as I wanted — bigger than me — and actually walk inside it. The computer doesn't run out of bricks. What really clicked was that it's deterministic: it does exactly what I tell it to. When something breaks, it's not an unreliable machine being unfair to me — it's a bug I wrote, and fixing it is on me. Physical and financial limits you can't reason with. A bug, you can.

But then, being a player stopped being enough, so I taught myself to build the things I wished existed — and I've been building ever since. It's not a career path so much as a habit of chasing whatever catches my interest: flight sims, a chess AI that evolves its own strategy, and tools I actually use — Neuronet, a personal knowledge system, and a past-paper tracker built to log my GCSE scores and how many papers I had left. Equilibrium is the largest of them, a world I keep expanding — but honestly, every project is just a castle I once couldn't afford to build :D
